\section*{One paragraph abstract}
The work asks when a chiral two dimensional transport response $Z=\bar\eta+i\eta_H$ is constrained to the circle anchored by its parity symmetric value and whether that constraint can survive beyond molecular chaos. Four results are separated. First, the deterministic HX89 endpoint automaton has an exact collision dressed reversor and an all time echo, supported by a complete \HXGlobalStates{} state certificate on the $2\times2$ six channel torus. Second, the published molecular chaos coefficients of the chiral FHP model lie exactly on a transport circle and satisfy a complex inverse shear eigenvalue relation \cite{Allocca2026}. Third, for any nonzero measured response, anchored circle membership is equivalent to constancy of $\RePart(1/Z)$; $C_6$ symmetry gives the shear normal form $aI+bJ$ but does not force the dissipative coordinate $a$ to be constant. Fourth, an exact stationary positive dissipation Ornstein Uhlenbeck Green Kubo family provides both rigid and nonrigid correlated classes, proving that symmetry and positivity alone do not protect the circle. The open problem is whether the fully correlated chiral FHP response obeys the inverse response rigidity condition under a specified finite size, time, frequency, or renormalization prescription.

\section*{Central equations}
With $Z=x+iy$, $Z_0=x_0>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=|Z|^2-x_0\operatorname{Re}Z.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Delta}{|Z|^2}=1-x_0\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac1Z\right),
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Re}\left(\frac1Z\right)=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac1{Z_0}\right).
\end{equation}
If $1/Z=a+ib$ and $a_0=1/x_0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=\frac{a_0-a}{a_0(a^2+b^2)}.
\end{equation}
This is the complete membership classifier for the specified anchored circle.

For the published zero field chiral FHP molecular chaos formulas, define $D=\rho(1-\rho/6)^3$, $d=p-1/2$, $A=1/(2D)$, and $u=(2\sqrt3/3)d$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \bar\eta=\frac{A}{1+u^2},\qquad
 \eta_H=-\frac{Au}{1+u^2},
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
 \bar\eta^2+\eta_H^2=A\bar\eta.
\end{equation}
The corresponding shear eigenvalue obeys $Z_B=-1/(4\lambda^*)$ in the repository convention.

\section*{Three strongest objections}
\subsection*{1. The circle is only complex inversion}
Complex inversion mapping a vertical line to a circle is classical. The work does not claim otherwise. The contribution is the exact anchored response classifier in terms of the measured inverse response, the symmetry boundary that shows $C_6$ does not force rigidity, and the explicit correlated witness on the nonrigid side. The limitation is that the general geometry of Mobius maps is prior mathematics, not a new result here.

\subsection*{2. Hexagonal symmetry should force the result}
It does not. Solving the commutant of the two component shear representation gives $aI+bJ$. Reflection permits arbitrary even functions in $a(d)$ and odd functions in $b(d)$. The OU counterexample chooses a positive even drift $a=a_0+\epsilon d^2$ and leaves the circle. A stronger microscopic principle could still protect a particular model, but it has not been proved for correlated chiral FHP.

\subsection*{3. The counterexample is not the FHP automaton}
Correct. It is not used to predict the FHP outcome. It proves that stationarity, positive dissipation, reflection covariance, and the two component rotational form are insufficient by themselves. A model specific FHP proof therefore needs additional structure.

\section*{Why not alternatives?}
\textbf{Why not fit circles directly?} A short noisy arc can fit many circles. The anchored inverse response criterion tests the exact relation and points directly to dissipative drift.

\textbf{Why not use local curvature alone?} A quartic dissipative drift has the rigid achiral curvature but leaves the circle away from the anchor.

\textbf{Why not decide from the full collision spectrum?} A Green Kubo response is a resolvent matrix element. Eigenvalues are insufficient without projectors and observable overlaps unless an exact reduced shear block and its normalization are identified.

\textbf{Why not call the defect relation a Ward identity?} No symmetry or conservation law has been shown to enforce constant $\RePart(1/Z)$. Established viscosity Ward identities have a derivation from symmetry generators or conservation laws \cite{Bradlyn2012}.
